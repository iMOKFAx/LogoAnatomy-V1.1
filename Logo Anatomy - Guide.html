Logo Anatomy is a panel for Adobe Illustrator. It draws how a logo is built (the circles its curves follow, the points that line up, its angles, its mirror axis and its clear space), then lists the small spots that are almost right, so you can decide what to clean up.

Try it on this page

  1. Pick a logo. Choose a sample, or press Upload SVG… to use your own (in Illustrator: File › Export › Export As › SVG).
  2. Read the guide on the artboard. Turn each kind on or off in Guides. Main guides shows the important lines only; All guides shows everything.
  3. Hover a note in Improvements: a ring shows the spot, and a dashed line shows where it should sit.
  4. Decide: Fix one spot, Fix all the safe ones, or Keep it when you meant it. Undo fixes brings the logo back.
  5. Export the guide as SVG or PNG for a presentation or a case study.

The samples have small flaws on purpose

  • Halo: the small dot has one bent handle, so its curve has a kink. Its ring is 100 / 62, almost the golden ratio: look at the ratio labels.
  • Leaf: the two sides are radius 140 and 137.6, almost the same size.
  • Peak: the snow cap is turned 0.8°, a ray is 29.2° instead of 30°, and its edges sit near clean angles without hitting them.

This page and the panel

Both use the same engine and the same rules. Here nothing is saved. In Illustrator, Fix moves the real points, and Ctrl+Z undoes it.

Logo Anatomy points out what is almost aligned, almost mirrored or almost round. It never decides for you: sometimes breaking symmetry makes a logo better. Trust your eye, and fix only what you mean to.

Every kind of guide has its own color and its own sub-layer in Illustrator, so you can show or hide each one.

Construction circles

The circle each curve follows, with a cross on its center. One circle per curve, drawn only when it really hugs the curve. A dashed circle means the curve is almost a circle, but wanders.

Ratio labels

Each circle's size against the biggest one (1). Sizes on the golden ratio get a φ: .618 φ, .382 φ, .236 φ. A logo built on a few related sizes feels calm and deliberate.

Alignment lines

Two or more points on exactly the same level or upright line. With Main guides, the 16 strongest lines (the ones shared by more points and parts) are shown.

Text line (tagline)

Small letters in one row are read as text: you get the baseline (solid), the x-height and the cap height (dashed), not a line through every letter.

Angles

Straight edges that are not level or upright, with their angle. Parallel edges share one number. Main guides shows up to 8 directions, the longest edges first.

Mirror axis

Drawn only when the whole logo is mirrored left to right. If one point is off its mirror partner, you get a note.

Box & center

The logo's outer box, its size in points, and its center lines.

Clear space

The empty space to keep around the logo: x = ¼ of its height on every side. Handy for a brand guideline.

Improvement dots

Red: a spot with a Fix. Orange: a tip. A red dashed line marks an edge that is almost level or upright.

Anchor points (only on this page) shows every point and handle, like the Direct Selection tool.

Each note says what it found and how far off it is. Only parts bigger than 5% of the logo are judged; tiny details and taglines are left alone.

The kinds of notes

  • FixRed: a small, safe change. Every fix is tried on a copy first and measured.
  • Bigger changeThe fix moves more than about 1% of the logo. You can still press Fix; Fix all leaves it to you.
  • Reshapes a curveMaking two circles the same size. Never part of Fix all.
  • tipOrange: the tool will not change it. It is a design choice, or a fix would move too much or add a bend in a smooth curve. Adjust it by hand, using the guide.

The safety rules

  • A fix never moves anything more than 8% of the logo.
  • A fix never puts a bend where the outline was smooth.
  • Moves are measured from the logo as it was read, so many small fixes cannot add up to a big jump.

The buttons

  • Fix: changes only that spot.
  • Fix all: fixes every safe note, one fix per point at a time. It skips bigger changes and curve reshapes, and tells you what is left for you to decide.
  • Keep it: you meant it. The note is hidden for this logo; show them again brings it back.
  • Undo fixes: back to the logo as it was when you pressed Make Guide. In Illustrator, Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z) works too.

Why a note stays

Tips and bigger changes are never fixed for you, so they stay in the list until you fix them by hand or press Keep it. That is on purpose: they are design decisions.

The line under Make Guide always says what was read and what was left out, and why.

Left out on its own

  • A background: a plain rectangle around the logo, many times bigger than it.
  • Parts far away from the logo, like a stray copy off to the side.
  • Invisible paths with no fill and no stroke (in Illustrator).
  • The shape of a clipping mask (the artwork inside it is read).

Read, but not judged

  • A tagline: small letters in a row get a baseline and height lines, and no notes.
  • Tiny details under 5% of the logo (dots, grain, small spokes).

Not read: prepare these first

  • Live text: Type › Create Outlines.
  • Effects and appearance strokes: Object › Expand Appearance.
  • Images: Image Trace, then Expand.

Select only the logo

If something big is selected with it (an artboard frame, a mock-up), the logo looks small next to it and most of its parts count as "tiny". The tool removes a plain background by itself, but selecting only the logo always gives the best guide.

Install

  1. Close Illustrator and unzip LogoAnatomy-v1.1.zip.
  2. Windows: double-click Install-Windows.bat.
    Mac: right-click Install-Mac.command › Open. If macOS blocks it: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway.
  3. Open Illustrator: Window › Extensions › Logo Anatomy.

The installer turns on PlayerDebugMode, the standard setting that lets Illustrator load panels installed outside Adobe Exchange. Nothing else changes.

Use

  1. Select the logo with the Selection tool (V) and press Make Guide.
  2. The guide is drawn on a locked layer called Logo Anatomy, on top of your artwork, with one sub-layer per kind. The checkboxes show or hide them without drawing again.
  3. Click a note: Illustrator selects that point and centers the view on it.
  4. Remove guide deletes the layer. Your logo is never on it.

To use Fix, the logo's own layer must not be locked.
